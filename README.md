# Build Housing Boston

*Build Housing Boston*: new housing being proposed, approved and built around Boston, at buildhousing.org: a map of it, then a list for each town, newest news first. A project opens in a panel (a sheet on a phone), as Pushpin's listings do, with what its source says about it: its dates, cost, size, developer and description, our note, and for Boston's projects the rendering from its page on bostonplans.org, which each build looks up only for projects it hasn't seen before (`images` in `projects.json`). Its address (`?project=boston-4930`) links straight to it. A page carries none of the panels' details: they're in `panels.json`, the same for every page, asked for as a project is about to be opened (when the reader first points at a row, or once the page is quiet). The map's library, a megabyte, is fetched only as the map comes near the window. Over the map, a Recently updated line turns over the newest changes, as Pushpin's Just announced does, and leads to Recent updates (`/recent/`): every project with something that happened in the last `RECENT_DAYS` days (90; the cities refresh their lists every few weeks or months), newest first, each saying what: a status seen to change ("Proposed → Approved", or "Newly listed"), a Boston filing or board approval, an update in MassBuilds, or our own note. The map is MapLibre's (from cdnjs), on OpenFreeMap's dark vector tiles, which need no key; the browser draws their roads and names itself, so they're sharp on any screen. The map draws every project at the zooms a reader starts at, so a town with little going on looks that way because it is; only further out, where the whole region is in view, does it leave the smaller ones out (40 homes or more, then 120 at its farthest), with a note in its corner saying so. A search, Recent updates and Large projects always show every one. Large projects (`/large/`) lists those of `LARGE_HOMES` homes or more (300) across every town, biggest first, each with a bar of four steps (proposed, approved, under construction, complete) showing how far along it is. Have your say (`/have-your-say/`) lists Boston's open comment periods and upcoming public meetings, soonest first: each comment period from its project's page on bostonplans.org ("Comment period ends Sep 30, 2026"), read each build for projects under review or filed in the last 120 days; each meeting from the Planning Department's calendar feed, whose descriptions link to the projects they're about. A project's panel lists its own in a Have your say box, its row carries a tag with the soonest, and a line under Recently updated shows the soonest of all. Cambridge's are its Planning Board's meetings, from its table of what's scheduled for each, whose items name the case numbers (PB-410) the development log gives for each project; its table has no times, so those say only their day. The towns MassBuilds covers publish neither, and their pages say so. Each town has a page of its own (`/somerville/`). The site's pages link to each other from its root (`/recent/`), as Pushpin's do, so `python3 -m http.server --directory dist/housing` serves it as it's published. A project's address is its panel's, on whichever page it's opened: `?project=boston-4930` (an older `#boston-4930` link still opens it), which its name in any list links to and its panel's Copy link copies. Search engines are welcome: every page has a heading of its own (the header says the same in its own way), a description and a canonical address; a town's page says a few lines about its town; each page carries what it is in JSON-LD (the site, the page, a town's projects as a list, and Have your say's meetings as events, with when each starts and where to join); and `sitemap.xml` lists them all. A link to a project (`/p/boston-4930/`, which its panel's Copy link copies) shows the project's own rendering, name and description, and whoever opens it lands on its panel; a link to any other page shows `share.png` (the house and the statuses' rings on the site's black, drawn by `make_icons.py` with the icons), with the page's own title and description as its words. Every page ends in a footer, as Pushpin's: its pages, each town (`#town-somerville` opens that town's list on the home page) and the sources. The site's mark is Tabler Icons' "building-community" (MIT), beside its name in the header; the icons in `housing/static`, and the picture a link shows, are drawn from that same path by `python3 -m housing.make_icons`, which strokes it as Tabler does.

- Boston's projects come from the Planning Department's map of projects under Article 80 review (anything over about 20,000 square feet or 15 homes); Cambridge's from its quarterly development log (50,000 square feet or 10 homes). The towns around them (`INNER_RING`: Somerville, Brookline, Medford and the rest) come from MAPC's [MassBuilds](https://www.massbuilds.com/), which MAPC's staff and the towns' planners keep up, some towns far more often than others, so each of those lists says when its data was last updated, in amber after six months. MassBuilds' "planning" (filed, approved or not) shows as proposed, and its "stalled" flag as stalled. Only projects with homes are listed, and finished ones only from 2020 on (`COMPLETE_SINCE`).
- Each project's status is one of proposed, approved, under construction, complete or stalled. A project's date is the latest of its filing, its approval, the day a build first saw its status change (each build compares with the last one's `projects.json`) and the date on our own edit. The first build has nothing to compare with, so Cambridge's projects, which have no dates of their own, go undated until they change.
- To correct a project, note something about it or add one the cities don't list, add a block to `housing/edits.txt` (its top explains how) and push; the page rebuilds at once. An edit that can't be used is reported in the build's log and skipped.
- Each project's panel has a Suggest a correction button, which opens a pre-filled issue titled *Note <project id>* with the note's fields under it. `housing-notes.yml` applies the repo owner's straight to `edits.txt` (`housing/notes.py`), replies, closes it and redeploys; anyone else's is labelled *correction* and left for the owner to judge, the site saying nothing until they note it themselves.
- Changes to MassBuilds' projects can go back to MassBuilds too, where anyone with an account can propose an edit or flag a record for MAPC to review. `python3 -m housing.upstream` lists what our edits would change there, each with its MassBuilds page, and whether it can go as an edit (MassBuilds takes one only if it leaves the whole record valid) or needs a flag. With `--send`, and `MASSBUILDS_EMAIL` and `MASSBUILDS_PASSWORD` in the environment (never in the repo), it posts each: an edit or a flag, both of which a MassBuilds moderator sees before anything changes there. It's run by hand, never by a build, since it posts publicly under that account; `housing/sent.txt` keeps what's gone up, so nothing goes twice.


## Running it

- `housing/`: the housing tracker. `build.py` reads Boston's and Cambridge's lists of development projects, and MassBuilds for the towns around them; `edits.txt` holds our own changes to them and projects they don't list.

- `python3 -m housing.build` writes `dist/housing`; `python3 -m http.server --directory dist/housing` serves it as it's published. `python3 -m unittest` runs the tests, against saved samples of each source in `tests/fixtures/housing`.

## Publishing

`.github/workflows/build.yml` runs the tests, builds the site, and replaces this repo's `gh-pages` branch with it, which GitHub Pages serves at buildhousing.org. It checks hourly and rebuilds when the last build is six hours old, or at once after a push to `main` or a "Run workflow". `shared/` is the page shell it shares with Pushpin and the newsfeed, copied from [grahamhagenah/news](https://github.com/grahamhagenah/news), where this lived until November 2026 (its history is there).
